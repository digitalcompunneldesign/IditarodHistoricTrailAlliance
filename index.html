This is the
Iditarod Trail

Not one path but a system of winter trails — some first worn by Alaska Native travelers, others blazed for the gold rush, all of them still in use. Here it is in four parts.

Before
you go

Most of the Iditarod is remote winter country. A few rules keep you, the trail and its history safe.

Do: Check conditions with the land manager first
Don’t: Never assume river ice is safe
Do: Dog teams have the right of way
Do: Tell someone your route and return date
Don’t: Do not camp on private or Native land without permission
Do: Carry gear for −40° and wind
Don’t: Leave artifacts and historic sites where they are
Do: Snowmachines slow down to pass
Do: Pack out everything you pack in
Don’t: Do not cross avalanche slopes after storms
